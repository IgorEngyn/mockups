Frequently Asked Questions

Questions about choosing counsel, legal fees and getting started with medical malpractice in New Jersey.

New Jersey malpractice litigation runs on rules that have no New York equivalent, and an attorney who works mainly across the river can lose a strong case on procedure alone. Claims are filed in the Superior Court of New Jersey, Law Division, in the county where the care was provided.

Two requirements dominate. First, the Affidavit of Merit under N.J.S.A. 2A:53A-27: within 60 days of the defendant's answer, extendable to 120 days for good cause, the plaintiff must serve a sworn statement from an appropriately licensed expert confirming a reasonable probability that the treatment fell outside acceptable professional standards. Failure to comply generally results in dismissal with prejudice. Second, the Patients First Act (N.J.S.A. 2A:53A-41) requires specialty matching — the expert must generally practice in the same specialty as the defendant, and must be board certified where the defendant is.

Timing: the general limitations period is two years (N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2), subject to New Jersey's discovery rule. For birth-related injuries, a claim on behalf of the child may generally be brought before the child's 13th birthday under N.J.S.A. 2A:14-2(b). Where a public entity or public hospital is involved, the Tort Claims Act generally requires notice within 90 days.

Ask about affidavit practice, expert relationships, and trial record. General information, not legal advice.

No upfront cost. New Jersey caps contingent fees by Court Rule 1:21-7:

  • 33 1/3% on the first $750,000 recovered
  • 30% on the next $750,000
  • 25% on the next $750,000
  • 20% on the next $750,000
  • Above $3,000,000, by application to the court

Where the client is a minor or an incapacitated person and the case settles without trial, the fee is generally limited to 25% of the net recovery — a rule that matters in most birth injury and pediatric cases.

What affects the total:

  • Case expenses: expert reviews across multiple specialties, complete records, life-care planning, economic analysis
  • Whether expenses are deducted before or after the fee is calculated
  • Whether the matter settles, is tried, or is appealed
  • Health insurance, Medicare and Medicaid liens, and benefit-protection planning through structured settlements or special needs trusts
  • Required court approval of any minor's settlement
