How Fluorescent Light Ballast Disposal Works

A ballast controls the current that powers a fluorescent lamp. When fixtures are removed during an LED retrofit or a building cleanout, ballasts and lamps turn into two waste streams with different rules. Ballasts are sorted by label, age and condition because some older units contain PCBs (polychlorinated biphenyls) in their capacitors, while fluorescent lamps contain mercury.

Counting and packing ballasts, lamps, fixtures and any batteries separately makes a retrofit far easier to document. Modern Waste Solutions, operating in Sacramento since 1999, plans every ballast pickup around these categories.

Identifying Ballasts That May Contain PCBs

According to the U.S. EPA, ballasts manufactured through 1979 may contain PCBs. Non-PCB ballasts produced between 1979 and 1998 had to carry a "No PCBs" label, so any unlabeled ballast taken from an older fixture should be treated as suspect. Sort ballasts into three groups ahead of pickup:

GroupHow to recognize itHow it is handled
Labeled non-PCB"No PCBs" printed on the housingStandard ballast recycling stream
Unlabeled or suspectNo "No PCBs" label, unknown age, or taken from older fixturesSet apart and reviewed before pickup
Leaking or damagedOily residue, staining, or a cracked or swollen housingIsolated in its own container and routed separately

EPA rules under the Toxic Substances Control Act set specific disposal requirements for leaking PCB ballasts, so these are packed and routed apart from the rest of the load. Never open or cut a ballast to inspect its capacitor; photos of the labels and manufacture dates are enough for our review.

Handling Fluorescent Lamps

Fluorescent tubes, compact fluorescent lamps and other lamps with added mercury are classed as universal waste under California's DTSC universal waste rules and cannot be thrown in the trash. Because a broken lamp releases mercury, intact packaging is the priority. Keep tubes in their original boxes or in lamp containers, label every container, and count lamps separately from ballasts. List high-intensity discharge and other lamp types on the quote request so each can be routed correctly.

Who Uses Our Ballast Disposal Service
  • Facilities managers switching commercial buildings from fluorescent to LED lighting.
  • Electrical contractors and maintenance crews removing fixtures as part of a job.
  • Schools, healthcare sites and industrial facilities with ballasts or lamps in storage. The EPA publishes dedicated guidance on PCB ballasts in school buildings.
  • Property managers clearing lighting waste across a portfolio of buildings.

Retrofits often uncover other material at the same time, such as fixtures, exit-sign and emergency-light batteries, or old lighting controls. Add them to your request: batteries go to our battery recycling stream, and electronic controls can be added to a business electronics recycling pickup.

Sorting and Storing Removed Ballasts on a Retrofit
  • Sort ballasts into the three groups as fixtures come down, while the labels are still easy to read.
  • Place leaking units in a separate closed container and keep it away from the rest of the load.
  • Keep lamps whole and boxed so they do not break during transport.
  • Record counts or approximate weights for each group, and photograph the labels.
  • Store everything indoors or under cover until pickup.
